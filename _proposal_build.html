About this document

This report has two jobs. First, it states the proposed solution — the problem being solved, the design principle behind it, and the architecture that follows from that principle. Second, it gives an accurate, source-verified account of what is already implemented, so that the proposal can be read against reality rather than intent.

Every count, formula, weight and threshold quoted here was read directly from the code in this repository, not from its documentation. Where the existing documentation and the code disagree, the code is treated as authoritative and the discrepancy is recorded in Section 9. No code was modified in the preparation of this document.

The system is built and running end to end.

The core idea

Conventional emergency tooling assumes information is reliable and treats disagreement as an error state. In real disaster response, disagreement is the normal state: a field officer reports a bridge passable at 06:00, a community radio operator reports it swept away at 08:30, and a satellite pass from 04:45 shows rising water. Our design principle is that uncertainty is a first-class data type, not an afterthought. Every observation is retained, weighted by source reliability, self-reported confidence and exponential freshness decay, and fused into a single estimate that carries its own confidence and an explicit conflict flag. Nothing downstream — priority scoring, route planning, resource assignment — is allowed to forget how well-supported its inputs were.

What is implemented

All five analytical engines are complete and deterministic: evidence fusion with conflict detection, criticality and priority scoring, a NetworkX accessibility graph with bridge-capacity and blocked-edge handling, a greedy-plus-local-improvement resource optimiser with a nearest-resource baseline for comparison, and an event-driven simulation engine that re-runs the entire pipeline after every event. These sit behind a FastAPI service exposing 24 operations, a React 19 operational dashboard with a live Leaflet map, and a 14-tool MCP server that lets IBM Bob answer operational questions from real backend state rather than canned text.

The differentiator

When bridge B1 receives two contradictory field reports fifteen minutes apart — partially blocked at confidence 0.82, then open at confidence 0.50 — the system does not silently pick a winner. It computes weighted support for each competing value, finds the runner-up holds at least 30 % of total evidence weight, flags the subject as conflicting, lowers the confidence propagated to every downstream consumer, and emits a recommendation to verify in the field before dispatching. That conflict then depresses B1's contribution to priority scoring and inflates the effective routing cost of the road it carries. The uncertainty travels with the decision.

What this is not

This is a decision-support layer, not an autonomous dispatcher. The optimiser emits recommendations with rationale; DecisionExplanation.human_verification_required defaults to True in the domain model and is never overridden anywhere in the codebase. It is also a closed simulation: there is no live sensor, satellite or telephony ingestion, and every entity, population figure and event in the scenario is synthetic.

2The Problem

2.1 Information fragmentation is the first casualty

When a major disaster strikes a remote mountainous region, reliable information fails before anything else does. Floods, landslides and earthquakes simultaneously sever roads, cut power, disable cellular infrastructure and displace the very personnel responsible for reporting conditions. What arrives at an emergency operations centre is not a picture of the situation; it is a stream of fragments that disagree with each other and with themselves over time. No single report is complete, no single source is authoritative, and the synthesis has to happen in minutes.

The coordinator's actual task is therefore not data retrieval. It is inference under time pressure across three simultaneous unknowns: what is true (which of these conflicting reports to believe), what is reachable (whether the route implied by a dispatch decision still exists), and what is worth doing next (which of seven deteriorating sites earns the one remaining ambulance). Each of those three is tractable in isolation. Together, under fatigue, they are where allocation decisions go wrong.

2.2 The motivating scenario

Our reference case is modelled on the geography and logistics of a Nepali river valley: a densely settled corridor flanked by steep ridgelines, served by a small number of routes whose bridges are single points of failure. A monsoon flood triggers overlapping crises across five villages, a district hospital, two health posts, two schools, a shelter and a hydropower station within a three-hour window. Eight response units — three rescue teams, two ambulances, an engineering team, a supply vehicle and a medical unit — must be allocated while bridges fail, roads block, a rescue team goes mechanically unavailable, and field reports contradict one another.

Synthetic data disclaimer

All entities, coordinates, population figures, events and benchmark values in this project are entirely synthetic and generated for simulation purposes. They are proportioned to reflect realistic operational complexity but do not reconstruct any actual event, location or casualty data.

2.3 Why existing tooling falls short

ApproachWhat it does wellWhere it breaks
GIS dashboards Visualise incident pins, asset locations and resource positions on a map They display, they do not reason. A dashboard can show a bridge status of “unknown”; it cannot weigh the age of that report against the reliability of its source and the urgency of the destination. All synthesis is externalised to an operator who is already saturated.
Conversational assistants Answer questions and retrieve records in natural language A chatbot waits to be asked. It cannot monitor a continuously updating state, notice that an approved route has become impassable, or recompute allocations when a new report lands. Disaster response cannot wait to be asked.
Commercial routing engines Compute fast, accurate shortest paths under normal conditions They optimise distance and time, not accessibility under degradation. They do not model a road rated passable at hour one and impassable by hour three, and they have no representation for a route that is technically open but operationally unsafe because its only bridge has a structural confidence of 0.4.
Unaided human prioritisation Contextual judgement, local knowledge, accountability — irreplaceable Across seven or more simultaneous incidents with degrading information quality, cognitive load, anchoring bias and fatigue produce allocations that systematic analysis measurably improves. The goal is to support this judgement, not replace it.

2.4 The gap we address

The gap is not a shortage of data or of technology. It is the absence of a layer that can do five things at once:

  1. Fuse conflicting, uncertain, time-decaying observations into a coherent picture that carries its own confidence
  2. Model infrastructure accessibility dynamically, as conditions and the evidence about them evolve
  3. Optimise constrained allocation across competing priorities and incompatible resource types
  4. Replan continuously as each new observation arrives, rather than producing a single plan
  5. Explain itself in natural language, with every recommendation traceable to the observations that produced it

3Proposed Solution

An evidence-aware operational decision engine. It sits between raw observation streams and the coordinator, and its only job is to convert fragments into defensible, confidence-weighted, continuously updated guidance.

3.1 Design principle

The system does not wait for a complete and consistent picture before producing recommendations. It treats every observation as partial evidence, quantifies what is known and unknown, and produces the best available guidance given the current information state — while making its confidence and its reasoning legible to the human who must act. This distinguishes it from passive dashboards, which present data without reasoning, and from autonomous agents, which act without adequate oversight.

Two consequences follow directly from that principle, and both are implemented. First, confidence and accessibility are priority inputs, not annotations: a critically affected village reachable only by a low-confidence route scores differently from an identical village with a confirmed clear route. Second, the analytical core is deterministic: evidence weighting, scoring, routing and assignment are closed-form or algorithmic, so every number is reproducible and auditable. The language model is confined to the interface.

3.2 The decision pipeline

Seven stages run in sequence. Stages 2 through 6 re-execute in full after every single event, which is what makes the replanning continuous rather than periodic.

1
Observation intake
Reports arrive from nine source classes — satellite, drone, field report, emergency call, hospital, school, sensor, authority report, simulation — each carrying a timestamp, a self-reported confidence, a subject entity and an observation type. Nothing is discarded and nothing is trusted unconditionally. Observations are persisted with full provenance.
2
Evidence fusion with uncertainty
Observations sharing a subject and attribute are fused into one estimate. Each contributes source_reliability × confidence × freshness, where freshness decays exponentially with age at a rate tuned per observation type. Competing values are canonicalised into comparable categories, so impassable, collapsed and closed all count as support for blocked. The winner is the value with the greatest weighted support, and it carries a derived confidence rather than inheriting one.
3
Conflict detection
If the runner-up value holds at least 30 % of total evidence weight, the subject is flagged conflicting, the specific dissenting observation IDs are attached, and a field-verification recommendation is generated. The flag then reduces the confidence factor feeding priority scoring, so an unresolved conflict measurably changes the plan instead of merely appearing in a log.
4
Situation assessment and scoring
Fused evidence drives two distinct scores per site. Criticality captures intrinsic severity from population, service class, time sensitivity, isolation and damage. Priority then folds in what can actually be done, adding urgency, impact, accessibility and evidence confidence. Keeping them separate is deliberate: a site can be critically affected yet correctly deprioritised this hour because no route to it clears the confidence bar.
5
Dynamic accessibility graph
The road network is a directed graph whose edge costs encode current condition, not just distance. Blocked edges are removed from consideration entirely; unknown, high-risk and partially-blocked edges carry multiplicative penalties. Bridge state dominates the road that carries it — a blocked bridge blocks its road outright — and vehicle weight class is enforced against bridge capacity, so a heavy ambulance is refused a medium-rated crossing that a rescue team may use.
6
Constrained resource optimisation
Tasks are generated from the scored situation, then matched to resources under three hard constraints: capability compatibility, resource availability, and route feasibility. Pairs whose route does not exist are eliminated before scoring, which is how blocked-route violations are structurally prevented rather than merely penalised. A greedy pass produces an initial one-to-one assignment; pairwise swaps then refine it where they beat the incumbent by more than 5 % on priority-weighted travel time.
7
Explanation and human confirmation
Every assignment carries the priority score that motivated it, the constraints that shaped it, the alternatives that were rejected, and a confidence value. IBM Bob renders these as prose on request. The recommendation is advisory by design; the human decides.

3.3 The AI / deterministic boundary

This boundary is the most important architectural commitment in the system, because it is what makes the output defensible after the fact.

Evidence quality assessment and scoring are never delegated to a language model. Bob's role is interface translation in both directions: turning a coordinator's question into a structured tool call, and turning a structured response into readable prose. If watsonx credentials are absent or the call fails, a deterministic provider generates the same summaries from templates and the analytical output is unchanged.

4.1 Replanning cycle

The single most important control-flow fact about the system: after every event, whether scripted or operator-injected, the backend runs the same four-stage pipeline before returning. There is no partial or incremental update path, which trades some efficiency for the guarantee that state is never internally inconsistent.

# app/engines/simulation.py — executed after every processed or injected event _process_event(evt) → create observation, mutate asset / road / bridge / resource state _fuse_all() → re-fuse every (entity, observation_type) group _recalculate_priorities()→ rescore all 13 non-infrastructure assets _generate_tasks() → discard and rebuild the task set from current state _run_optimizer() → new AI plan + baseline plan, persist decisions

4.2 State ownership

A module-level WorldState object is the authoritative in-memory store: assets, roads, bridges, resources, observations, the fused-evidence cache keyed by entity:observation_type, priorities, tasks, the current and baseline plans, simulated clock position, and the audit log. SQLite is the durable record for the entities, raw observations, decisions and the event script. Derived artefacts — fused evidence, priorities, tasks and plans — are recomputed rather than stored, which means a restart replays cleanly from the observation record.

Architectural note: the clock

The existing docs/architecture.md describes a tick loop advancing simulated time in configurable 15-minute intervals. The implementation is event-driven, not ticked: processing an event jumps the simulated clock directly to that event's time_offset_min. The 15-minute spacing is a property of the scenario file, not of a configured tick. The behaviour is equivalent for the scripted scenario and simpler to reason about, but the document should be corrected.

5.2 Evidence fusion engine — complete

Implements the full weighting chain. Freshness is exp(-λ × age_hours) with age measured against the simulated clock and floored at zero. Weight is the product of source reliability, the observation's own confidence, and freshness. Before competing values are compared they are canonicalised into ten categories, so semantically identical reports phrased differently accumulate support together instead of splitting it.

Fused confidence is the product of two terms: the fraction of total weight supporting the winning value, and the mean weight of the observations that support it. This is why a lone stale low-confidence report yields low confidence even though it is unopposed. Confidence is then banded into high / medium / low / very low for display. An empty observation set returns unknown at confidence 0.0 rather than raising.

Property worth knowing

Because the second term is a mean rather than a sum, corroborating duplicates do not raise confidence — three identical observations of weight w still yield roughly w. Additional independent sources raise confidence only by shrinking the competing fraction. This is conservative and safe, but the value should be read as a support score rather than a calibrated probability.

5.3 Graph accessibility engine — complete

A NetworkX DiGraph in which nodes are asset IDs and each road becomes two directed edges, so the 14 seeded roads yield 28 traversable edges. Routing builds a filtered edge subgraph that excludes three classes of edge — vehicle-restricted, bridge-capacity-insufficient, and blocked — then runs Dijkstra weighted by effective cost. Every excluded road is returned to the caller in blocked_alternatives, so the answer explains what was ruled out and not merely what was chosen. Infeasibility is a first-class return value carrying a reason string, never an exception.

Bridge state dominates the road that carries it. A blocked bridge forces its road to blocked at risk 1.0; a high-risk, unknown or partially-blocked bridge raises the road's risk to the corresponding penalty; and road confidence becomes the minimum of road and bridge confidence. Vehicle fit is enforced on an ordered capacity scale, so a heavy unit is refused a medium bridge while a medium unit is permitted.

5.4 Priority engine — complete

Both scores are implemented with weights read from configuration, and both weight sets sum to exactly 1.0 — an invariant asserted by a dedicated unit test rather than assumed. Static per-entity-type tables supply service criticality and time sensitivity for twelve entity types, with a 0.5 default for anything unrecognised. Population is normalised against a 10,000-person reference.

The derived inputs are all implemented and all evidence-driven: urgency takes the maximum over a mapping of evidence values (destroyed 1.0 down to damaged 0.60) and asset status; isolation risk reads confirmed isolation directly and treats a high-confidence blockage as 0.7; damage severity multiplies a per-value severity by that value's confidence and takes the maximum; and the confidence factor is mean evidence confidence reduced by 15 % per active conflict, defaulting to 0.3 where no evidence exists at all. Every score ships with a generated explanation string and a list of supporting factors, including an explicit conflict count.

Directional design choice

Accessibility and confidence are positive priority terms. An unreachable site therefore loses up to 0.15 of its score and a poorly-evidenced site loses up to 0.10. This encodes “prioritise what we can actually act on, with evidence we can actually trust” — and it is why criticality is kept separate, so the underlying need of a deprioritised site remains visible rather than being erased.

5.5 Resource optimiser — complete

Greedy assignment with pairwise local improvement. Eligibility requires both that the resource is available and that the task type appears in that resource type's capability list; the five-entry capability map is explicit, so an ambulance cannot be sent to an engineering task. Routing is performed per candidate pair using the vehicle class implied by the resource type, and pairs whose route is infeasible are dropped before scoring — structural prevention of blocked-route dispatch rather than a soft penalty.

Pairs are scored as priority × (0.4 + 0.6 × time_efficiency) where time efficiency decays linearly to zero at a 120-minute travel ceiling. The greedy pass takes pairs in descending score order with strict one-to-one matching. Local improvement then runs up to ten passes of pairwise swaps, accepting a swap only when it beats the incumbent by more than 5 % on priority-weighted travel time. Each assignment produces a DecisionExplanation carrying reasons, constraints, rejected alternatives and a confidence value.

A separate baseline_nearest_assignment implements the naive comparator: take tasks in priority order, give each the feasible resource with the shortest travel time, no refinement. This is what the benchmark measures against, and it is a genuine algorithm rather than a synthetic penalty applied to our own result.

5.6 Simulation engine — complete

Event-driven rather than ticked. Processing an event advances the simulated clock to that event's offset, creates the corresponding observation with full provenance, persists it, dispatches to a state-change handler, then runs the four-stage replanning pipeline. Ten event types have dedicated handlers covering bridge damage, conflicting bridge reports, confirmed bridge blockage, road blockage, alternate route opening, resource unavailability, hospital demand surge, school evacuation, satellite observation and confirmed village isolation.

Operator-injected events take the identical path, so anything the scripted scenario can do, a demo facilitator or Bob can do live. Task generation is idempotent: the task set is discarded and rebuilt every cycle with deterministic IDs, infrastructure entity types are excluded, and task type selection requires evidence confidence of at least 0.5 before falling back to asset status. Reset restores all entity state from the scenario file, clears observations, decisions and the audit table, and marks every scripted event unprocessed.

Two reset caveats found in code

Injected events persist as EVT-INJ-* rows and survive a reset, so they are replayed as if scheduled. And the in-memory audit log is not cleared on reset, so it accumulates across cycles. Neither breaks a demo run, but both should be tightened before the system is used for repeated measured comparisons.

5.10 Persistence and seeding — complete

Async SQLAlchemy 2.0 over aiosqlite with eight tables: assets, roads, bridges, resources, observations, decisions, sim_events and audit_log. Structured sub-objects are stored as JSON text columns. Schema creation runs at startup; seeding is idempotent and exits early if assets already exist, so repeated launches are safe. Derived state — fused evidence, priorities, tasks, plans — is intentionally not persisted, because it is cheaper and safer to recompute it from the observation record than to keep it in sync.

5.11 AI provider — complete with fallback

An abstract provider with three operations: situation summary, decision explanation and operator Q&A. The watsonx implementation lazily imports the IBM SDK and calls ibm/granite-3-8b-instruct at temperature 0.3. A deterministic implementation produces the same three outputs from templates and keyword routing over live state. Selection requires both an API key and a project ID to be present; failure at client construction or generation falls back to the deterministic provider transparently. The system is therefore fully functional with no credentials, which is how it is expected to be demonstrated.

6.2 Scoring

criticality = 0.25·population_impact // min(1, population / 10 000) + 0.25·service_criticality // static per entity type + 0.20·time_sensitivity // static per entity type + 0.15·isolation_risk // from accessibility evidence + 0.15·damage_severity // severity × confidence, max over evidence priority = 0.30·criticality + 0.25·urgency // max over evidence values and asset status + 0.20·impact // normalised affected population + 0.15·accessibility // max(0.1, 1 - route_risk) from CC1; 0 if unreachable + 0.10·confidence // mean evidence confidence × (1 - 0.15·conflicts)

Service criticality runs from hospital 1.00 and command centre 0.95 down through hydropower 0.85, health post 0.80, shelter 0.75, school 0.60 and village 0.55 to river 0.10. Time sensitivity is a parallel table topped by hospital 1.00 and health post 0.90. Both weight sets sum to exactly 1.0, and a unit test asserts it.

Integrity of the comparison

We checked specifically for synthetic inflation of our own result, because that is the usual failure mode of self-reported benchmarks. The baseline is a real nearest-resource algorithm run on identical state, and no multiplier is applied to either side in the returned output. “Critical” is defined structurally as hospital, health post and shelter entities — four sites in this scenario — and not chosen to flatter the result.

One honest asymmetry to disclose when presenting: mean decision confidence falls back to 0.5 for the baseline, because the baseline algorithm does not construct decision explanations at all. That metric should be read as a property of the AI plan only, not as a comparison.

Benchmark figures are deliberately absent from this document. They depend on how far the simulation has advanced, and quoting a number here would invite exactly the kind of unverifiable claim the project is designed to avoid. Run make benchmark and report what it returns.

8.2 What the integration tests prove

Twenty-one async tests drive the real application through an ASGI transport against a freshly seeded database. They cover every read endpoint, assert that priorities are returned in descending order, process a scripted event, inject a road blockage, inject a two-report bridge contradiction and assert the entity is flagged conflicting, run the optimiser, ask an operator question, reset the simulation, and finally run an end-to-end pass of reset, seven events, situation, route, optimise and Q&A. Verification gaps — stated plainly

The suite was not executed during preparation of this document. The environment available had no installable Python package manager, so the backend dependencies could not be provisioned and the 64-test claim is a count of defined test functions, verified by inspection, not a passing run. Anyone presenting this project should run make test and confirm.

Three known fragilities in the test setup, found by reading it: the integration tests share module-global world state and are therefore order-dependent; they assume the working directory is src/backend for the SQLite file; and they define a custom event_loop fixture, which is deprecated in pytest-asyncio 0.23 and removed in 1.x. Also unverified: CI runs no tests at all. The GitHub workflow validates file presence, YAML parsing, required submission fields and placeholder replacement — six structural checks with no build, lint, type-check or test step. Green CI on this repository does not indicate working code.

9Gaps, Drift & Limitations

This section exists because a proposal that overstates its own completeness is worth less than one that does not. Everything here was found by comparing the repository's documentation against its code.

9.1 Documented but not implemented

The architecture describes confirm / reject cards feeding accepted allocations back into state, and the dashboard is built around advisory plans, but there is no write endpoint. 9.3 Engineering and security notes

Security posture — disclose before any deployment

There is no authentication or authorisation on any endpoint. CORS is configured with a wildcard origin while also allowing credentials, and the state-mutating operations — POST /simulate/event, /simulate/reset, /optimize — are open to any caller that can reach the port. This is acceptable for a local single-operator demo and is not acceptable for the hosted deployment the demo materials anticipate. If the project is deployed publicly, this needs an auth layer and an origin allowlist first.

  • Unpinned dependencies. All eleven backend packages use open >= ranges, so two installs a month apart can resolve to different versions. The pytest-asyncio case in Section 8.2 is a concrete instance of this already causing latent breakage.
  • Hardcoded environment paths. The Makefile hardcodes /opt/conda/bin/python3 and uses Unix-only commands; mcp_config.json hardcodes devcontainer absolute paths. Neither resolves on a Windows checkout, so both need editing before a local run outside the original container.
  • Undeclared MCP dependency. src/mcp/ has no requirements file but needs httpx, which it inherits from the backend environment. Running the MCP server in isolation will fail.
  • watsonx SDK not declared. ibm-watsonx-ai is absent from requirements.txt, so the watsonx path requires a manual install. The deterministic fallback masks this, which is convenient but means the watsonx branch is unlikely to have been exercised.
  • Optimiser decision records go stale after a swap. Local improvement rewrites the allocations but returns the decision list untouched, so a persisted DecisionDB row can describe the pre-swap pairing. Since explainability is the project's central claim, this is a correctness bug in the part that matters most.
  • Frontend loose ends. recharts is installed but never imported, so the metrics panel is numeric tiles rather than charts. Timeline data is fetched every 5 s and never rendered. VITE_API_BASE_URL is read by the API client but is absent from .env.example, so a production build served outside the dev proxy has no working API base.
  • Benchmark writes to the working directory. benchmark_results.json is written to the process CWD as a side effect of running the benchmark endpoint.

9.4 Scope limitations, by design

  • Closed simulation. No live ingestion of sensor, satellite, telephony or weather feeds. Observations arrive only from the scenario script or manual injection.
  • Synthetic data throughout. Every entity, coordinate, population and event is invented. The benchmark measures algorithmic behaviour on a constructed scenario, not real-world outcome improvement.
  • Greedy rather than exact optimisation. Assignment is heuristic, not a solved integer program. At eight resources and a handful of tasks the gap is negligible, but the system cannot claim optimality.
  • Accessibility measured from a single origin. The accessibility term in priority scoring routes from the command centre only, so it reflects reachability from headquarters rather than from whichever resource would actually respond.
  • Confidence is not calibrated. As set out in Section 5.2, fused confidence is a support score. It should not be presented as a probability.
  • Map tiles require internet. The dark CARTO basemap will not load offline; the rest of the dashboard degrades gracefully.
  • Demo artefacts incomplete. Three screenshots exist. The demo video link and live demo URL are both placeholders, and CI's placeholder check does not catch either.

11.4 Summary judgement

Where the project stands

The analytical core is the strongest part of this submission and it is finished. Five engines implement a coherent, defensible thesis — that uncertainty belongs in the decision, not in a footnote — and they do it deterministically, with every weight traceable and every number reproducible. The MCP integration is genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative, and the dashboard makes the replanning visible rather than merely claimed.

The weaknesses are at the edges, not the centre: an unclosed human-in-the-loop write path, an audit trail that does not persist, a demo guide that describes a different system, and unpinned dependencies that put the test suite's reproducibility at risk. All four are small and well understood. None requires revisiting the design.
